## Findings

### P1-1 — Common and custodian acceptance still replaces production owners above the syscall boundary

The common matrix never executes any body in production `SystemCommonOps` (`scripts/native-qualification/common.py:276-597`), including source admission and `run_fixed_operation()` (`369-455`). Its test double returns a previously completed result dictionary (`test/outcome-two-recovery-portable.py:666-687`); that dictionary is generated by a separate launcher test once at `893-895`, not by the tested common transaction. This leaves common's held-source walk, Git authentication, private module loading, exact operation dispatch, and observation owner outside portable branch reachability.

The same matrix does not execute production `_custodian_main()` or `_custodian_worker()` (`scripts/native-qualification/common.py:1354-1463`). `CommonKernel.fork()` only returns a parent PID (`test/outcome-two-recovery-portable.py:578-583`), while `CommonSocket.send()` and `CommonKernel.server_cleanup()` directly invoke publication/cleanup helpers (`335-352`, `619-630`). Thus child release, listener setup, worker creation/registration, peer handling, and the production custodian loop are simulated above helper boundaries rather than run above syscall mocks.

Portable line tracing confirmed no body statement in `SystemCommonOps`, `_custodian_main`, or `_custodian_worker` is reached. This does not satisfy ADR 0093 sections 4 and 10's complete common/custodian production-state-machine requirement.

### P1-2 — D's parent owner runs, but both production child state machines remain unreachable

`production_lifecycle_case()` reaches `_qualify_admitted_fixed_process_lifecycle()` and `_run_lifecycle_case()` (`test/outcome-two-lifecycle-portable.py:1056-1162`), which is an improvement over ADR 0092. It still does not run production `_lifecycle_leader()` or `_lifecycle_descendant()` (`completion_trusted_runtime_launcher.py:3044-3214`). Instead, `ScriptedSocket.recv()/recvmsg()` fabricates transition, transfer, credential, and release packets (`test/outcome-two-lifecycle-portable.py:716-770`), and `ProductionLifecycleKernel.clone_pidfd()` only creates a modeled process record (`940-955`).

Consequently the leader's preregistration gates, descendant transfer sender, child-side failure settlement, and child-side release ordering can regress while every matrix row remains green. Callable lookups from fixture strings do not establish branch reachability. ADR 0093 sections 7 and 10 require the complete D production state machine, not only its parent parser/owner.

### P1-3 — A, B, and integration still synthesize production child protocols

A's `MappingOwnerOps.clone3_pidfd()` always returns the parent side and its fake pipe reads/writes synthesize helper registration, release, status, and reap (`test/outcome-two-mapped-closure-portable.py:277-445`). The child branch of production `_spawn_helper()` is not executed.

B and integration similarly exercise the parent `_coordinate_with_ops()`/`_run_tool_with_ops()` paths, but `_BIKernel.clone_pidfd()` and `_BISocket` fabricate issuance, namespace, transfer, boundary, and exit traffic (`test/outcome-two-trusted-launcher-portable.py:1700-2058`). Production `_worker_main()` and `_namespace_owner()` (`completion_trusted_runtime_launcher.py:1396-1537`, `1955-1995`) receive no body coverage.

C materially does recurse through its production child branch, and E materially runs `_sandbox_inner()` and `_sandbox_leader()` over its threaded syscall model. Those improvements do not cure A/B/integration. ADR 0093 section 10 requires every named production state machine above mocked native primitives; protocol fabrication at the child-owner boundary is non-accepting.

### P1-4 — A declared launcher corpus is silently unselected, and its local ledger remains nominal

`test/fixtures/outcome-two/launcher/outer-process-cases.jsonl` declares 19 `AT92-OUTER-01` rows against `_run_held_python_with_ops`, but that production symbol is absent at the reviewed head. The suite silently skips the entire corpus behind `if hasattr(module, "_run_held_python_with_ops")` (`test/outcome-two-trusted-launcher-portable.py:2195-2197`). None of those declared identities enters a selected, consumed, or oracle set.

Even if the symbol returned, `outer_process_corpus()` catches broad errors and then unconditionally adds each identity to `consumed` and `oracle` (`991-1017`) without requiring `ops.fired`, the declared sentinel, the exact rejection code, or cleanup-domain restoration. This is the nominal accounting defect ADR 0093 section 10 forbids. A removed route must have its corpus explicitly retired or absence-proved; a conditional skip cannot establish `declared = selected = consumed = oracle`.

### P2-1 — The AST readability gate still permits unlabeled positional security claims

The gate scans all required files, but defines claims only as call names ending in `Result`, `Observation`, `Receipt`, or `Evidence` (`test/outcome-two-portable.test.ts:121,179-180`). Security-bearing types with other names bypass it. Concrete accepted production examples include:

- twelve positional authority fields in `_SourceAdmission(...)` at launcher line 3489;
- ten positional generation claims in `_GenerationRow(...)` at launcher lines 547 and 563;
- positional `_ProcessLease(...)` construction at launcher lines 777 and 791;
- positional `_PrivateGenerationRow(...)` and `HelperLease(...)` construction in closure lines 1194 and 2314.

These are exactly the authority, generation, process, and ownership claims for which field-label readability matters. The semicolon and allocation scans are present, but the claim predicate is too narrow to enforce ADR 0093 section 11.
